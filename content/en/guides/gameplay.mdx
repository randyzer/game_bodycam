Bodycam is Reissad Studio's tactical multiplayer first-person shooter for Windows PC. The [Steam listing](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2406770/Bodycam/) describes a true body-camera view in Unreal Engine 5 and close-quarters matches where angles, communication and individual decisions matter. The [official website](https://www.playbodycam.com/en-US) emphasizes lens distortion, exposure changes and spatial audio as part of the game's presentation.

## The core experience

The camera perspective is central to how Bodycam looks and feels. The official site names motion blur, sensor noise and realistic lighting as visual choices, while its audio description highlights reverb and occlusion. These are developer descriptions, not a guarantee of a particular frame rate or of how every player will perceive the game.

Because visibility and sound matter in a tactical shooter, approach your first sessions as observation. Learn a route, keep track of a teammate's position and use the information you actually hear and see. These are general tactical suggestions, not claims about a hidden scoring system.

## Modes you can verify

The Steam summary explicitly names Deathmatch, Team Deathmatch and Wingman. Its fuller current description also mentions Body Bomb, Hardpoint, Gun Game and co-op Zombies. The available playlist and rules can shift during Early Access. Check the live in-game menu and the official news feed before relying on an older mode list.

For the survival side, see <GuideLink slug="guides/zombies">our Zombies overview</GuideLink>. For the developer's environmental design notes, read <GuideLink slug="guides/trenches">Trenches</GuideLink>.

## What this guide does not assume

An online PvP listing does not establish a dedicated single-player campaign, crossplay or a console release. We also cannot infer the best weapon from a trailer or a brief patch note. Those questions remain open until a current first-party source addresses the exact feature.
